# Scientific reading route

The operational question is whether hidden phases that persist across emissions can help a quantum receiver return a label absent from an unknown support. The same single-specimen density operator does not determine the answer: the repeated-copy source retains different phase correlations.

1. [Source model](model.md) fixes balanced support, cyclic phase order, exact known refresh blocks, unconditional success and hard acquisition cost.
2. [Persistence frontier](persistence-frontier.md) states the matching copy law and identifies its frozen, short-block and continuous-phase regimes.
3. [Phase-twirl converse](phase-twirl-converse.md) derives the occupation-residue common component, the exact classical-data comparison, and the lower bound against unrestricted collective processing.
4. [Coherent recovery](coherent-recovery.md) gives the attributed multiplicity-corrected extractor, exact success probability and charged schedule within refresh blocks.
5. [Implementation](implementation.md) accounts for occupation processing, protected workspace, rare-batch synthesis, source descriptions and preparation costs.
6. [Interpretation](interpretation.md) explains the temporal resource distinction and the limits of the sampling advantage.

The [related-work account](../references/related-work.md) identifies the original complement sampler, the inherited coupon-state simulator, phase-moment methods and the power extractor. Each proof page names its dependencies. The [finite verification](../verification/README.md) checks consequential identities by independent enumeration and small-state constructions, while the analytical proofs establish the asymptotic laws.

The symbol $`N`$ denotes the universe size, $`M=N/2`$ the support size, $`q`$ a fixed cyclic phase order, $`B`$ the known number of consecutive copies sharing one phase table, and $`T`$ a hard total copy budget. The parameter $`B`$ restricts the source, not receiver memory.
